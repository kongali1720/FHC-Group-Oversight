# FHC Investor Onboarding Policy

**Document ID:** FHC-IP-001  
**Version:** 1.0 — Draft for Management Review  
**Document Owner:** FHC Management / Finance  
**Approval Status:** Pending formal approval  
**Effective Date:** To be determined upon approval

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes a consistent and documented process for
receiving, reviewing, approving, and onboarding prospective investors
who may participate in eligible FHC group business opportunities.

The process is intended to support transparency, due diligence,
confidentiality, risk management, and compliance with applicable laws.

This document is an internal procedure. It is not an investment offer,
a promise of returns, a funding commitment, or evidence that any
investor or transaction has been approved.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to prospective investors and relevant FHC personnel
involved in investment discussions relating to:

- DA-B
- DA-RS
- DA-MI
- DA-Food
- HGA

The responsible legal entity, project owner, and contracting party must
be identified for each opportunity before formal commitments are made.
The meaning and legal identity of each unit must be confirmed by
management.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Business Development / Investor Relations
- Record the investor introduction and source of referral.
- Explain the proposed business opportunity using approved materials.
- Avoid promises of guaranteed returns, funding dates, or approvals.
- Submit investor information to the authorized internal review team.

### 3.2 FHC Finance
- Review the proposed funding amount, currency, purpose, timing, and
  expected impact on the relevant business plan.
- Assess the proposed funding structure with qualified advisers.
- Review the source and intended use of funds as required by policy.
- Record outstanding financial questions and required approvals.

### 3.3 Legal / Compliance
- Determine applicable due diligence and regulatory requirements.
- Review the investor's legal capacity, relevant ownership information,
  sanctions and screening requirements, and transaction documentation.
- Confirm whether external legal, tax, licensing, or regulatory advice
  is required.
- Escalate unresolved compliance concerns before any commitment.

### 3.4 Authorized Management
- Decide whether to proceed, request more information, or decline.
- Approve material commercial terms only within formally delegated
  authority.
- Ensure that required corporate approvals are recorded.

No role listed here automatically has authority to bind an FHC entity.
Authority must be established in the applicable Delegation of Authority.

## 4. Onboarding Process

### Stage 1 — Initial Registration
Record the prospective investor's name or legal entity name, country
of registration or residence where relevant, referral source, contact
details, opportunity, proposed amount and currency, and assigned
internal owner.

Collect personal information only when necessary and through an
approved secure channel.

### Stage 2 — Preliminary Assessment
Document the proposed investment purpose, business rationale,
anticipated structure, indicative timetable, and initial risks.

All figures and timelines must be identified as indicative unless
formally approved and supported by documentation.

### Stage 3 — Due Diligence
Subject to applicable law and risk level, obtain and review appropriate
documents, which may include:

- Corporate registration and constitutional documents.
- Evidence of identity and authority of authorized representatives.
- Beneficial ownership information where legally required.
- Source-of-funds and, where required, source-of-wealth information.
- Sanctions, politically exposed person, and other risk screening
  where applicable.
- Evidence of authority to invest or provide the proposed funding.
- Additional documents requested by Legal / Compliance.

Documents must be reviewed by authorized personnel. Receipt of a
document does not mean that its contents have been verified or that
the investor has been approved.

### Stage 4 — Risk and Compliance Review
Record the due diligence result, outstanding information, risk rating,
mitigations, and recommendation.

Any unresolved material identity, source-of-funds, sanctions,
fraud, regulatory, or ownership concern must be escalated before
the transaction proceeds.

Do not bypass required controls because of commercial urgency.

### Stage 5 — Business and Financial Review
The relevant business unit and FHC Finance assess:

- Business plan and use of proceeds.
- Funding requirements and cash-flow assumptions.
- Commercial terms and allocation of risks.
- Relevant licenses, permits, and regulatory considerations.
- Reporting obligations and proposed monitoring arrangements.
- Exit, repayment, or distribution terms, if applicable.

All projections must identify assumptions and must not be presented
as guaranteed outcomes.

### Stage 6 — Legal Documentation
Qualified legal advisers should determine the appropriate documents,
which may include a confidentiality agreement, term sheet, investment
agreement, subscription agreement, loan agreement, or other contract.

No document should be represented as executed or binding unless it
has actually been signed by authorized parties and all required
conditions have been satisfied.

### Stage 7 — Formal Approval
The designated approver must review the complete approval package,
including due diligence status, commercial terms, legal review,
financial analysis, risks, and required corporate approvals.

Record the decision as one of:
- Approved
- Approved subject to specified conditions
- Pending additional information
- Declined

Only authorized persons may communicate a final approval.

### Stage 8 — Funding and Verification
Funding instructions may be issued only through verified and approved
channels after required agreements and approvals are in place.

Verify beneficiary details independently using approved procedures.
Record the transaction reference and reconcile receipt through
authorized financial records.

Never treat a draft payment message, screenshot, transaction reference,
or unverified notification as proof that cleared funds have been
received. Do not request or store passwords, PINs, OTPs, private keys,
or online banking credentials.

### Stage 9 — Post-Onboarding Monitoring
Assign a relationship owner and reporting schedule. Monitor agreed
milestones, use of funds, reporting obligations, material changes,
risks, and contractual commitments.

Escalate missed obligations or material deviations and document
the resolution.

## 5. Required Onboarding Register

Maintain an access-controlled register containing, at minimum:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Investor ID | Unique internal reference |
| Investor / entity name | Legal name where applicable |
| Opportunity / unit | Relevant project or business unit |
| Internal owner | Responsible FHC contact |
| Proposed amount and currency | Indicative until approved |
| Due diligence status | Not started / In progress / Complete |
| Compliance status | Pending / Cleared / Escalated / Declined |
| Legal review | Pending / Complete / Not applicable with reason |
| Approval status | Pending / Conditional / Approved / Declined |
| Agreement status | Draft / Under review / Executed |
| Funding status | Not due / Expected / Received and reconciled / Exception |
| Outstanding actions | Owner and due date |
| Last review date | Date of latest documented review |

Do not place identity documents, bank account details, or other
restricted investor data in a public repository.

## 6. Confidentiality and Data Protection

Investor information must be collected for a defined business purpose,
restricted to authorized personnel, stored securely, and retained or
deleted according to applicable law and approved company policy.

Use a controlled document repository with appropriate access
permissions and audit logging. Do not commit confidential investor
files, KYC documents, bank statements, or signed confidential
agreements to a public Git repository.

## 7. Red Flags and Escalation

Escalate promptly if any of the following occurs:

- Identity or beneficial ownership cannot be reasonably established.
- Source-of-funds information is inconsistent or unsupported.
- A party requests that required due diligence be bypassed.
- Payment instructions change unexpectedly or cannot be independently
  verified.
- A party requests false confirmations, backdated documents, or
  misleading proof of funds.
- Transaction terms materially differ from approved documentation.
- A regulatory, sanctions, fraud, or conflict-of-interest concern
  remains unresolved.

Record objective facts, preserve relevant evidence, and restrict
disclosure to personnel who need to know. Do not make unsupported
accusations.

## 8. Records and Audit Trail

Retain the onboarding checklist, due diligence status, review notes,
approval records, executed agreements, funding reconciliation,
exceptions, and post-onboarding reviews in the designated secure
repository in accordance with applicable retention requirements.

Every material decision should identify the decision-maker, date,
supporting evidence, conditions, and any follow-up action.

## 9. Policy Review and Approval

The document owner should review this policy at least annually and
when there is a material change in law, business structure, risk,
or operating procedure.

Before this policy becomes effective, management must confirm:
- The responsible legal entity and document owner.
- The authorized approvers and applicable Delegation of Authority.
- The due diligence and data-protection procedures.
- The approved secure storage location and retention schedule.
- The effective date and formal approval record.
